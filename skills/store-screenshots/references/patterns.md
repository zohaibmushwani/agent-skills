# What high-performing screenshot sets do (studied from a set of top-chart and redesigned listings)
1. **One headline per frame, 2-4 words, huge.** Top third of the frame, heavy weight, tight leading. It is read before anything else. ("Snap your meal", "Track meals daily", "Just swipe".)
2. **Frame 1 is a poster, not a screen.** The promise plus a strong visual; little or no UI. It must work alone because most visitors never swipe.
3. **Show a fragment of UI, zoomed.** Instead of a small full phone, crop to the card/row/control that proves the headline and enlarge it; often one element "pops out" past the phone or frame edge as a floating card with a shadow.
4. **The set is one composition.** A word, shape, illustration or photo continues across frame borders, so the row reads as a panorama and invites swiping.
5. **One dominant brand colour field** across the set plus one contrasting accent (lime on dark, red with black, deep green with orange). Not a different colour per frame unless it is a planned sequence.
6. **Proof:** ratings laurels, "App of the Day", "1M+ users", press logos - only real ones.
7. **Human touches:** handwritten annotations ("done!", "planned!"), stickers, emoji, a mascot, a notification card, a timer - small, purposeful, one or two per frame.
8. **Category-appropriate art:** food photo cut-outs for nutrition, illustrated worlds for learning, poster/cover art for books and media, product cut-outs for shopping.

## Before -> after lessons
- Small phones with tiny UI and lowercase captions -> big type, cropped UI, colour field.
- Every frame a different idea of style -> one system (font, colour, framing) across the set.
- Generic stock emoji clutter -> one character or motif that belongs to the brand.
- Feature names ("Backup", "Restore") -> outcomes ("Your music is safe", "Restore anything instantly").
